Aerion · outbound account & contact generation · thought process

Everything an AI says about a company
could be invented. So nothing here is said by one.

A campaign brief goes in. An account list, a buying committee, a research brief and a personalised email come out. The interesting part is not the pipeline, it is the one rule the pipeline is built around, and the refusals that rule forces.

The question I started from

What would make a reviewer believe an account list they have never seen before?

Not fluency. A model writes a plausible list of Chilean mining companies in four seconds, and a plausible Head of Operations to go with it, and there is no way to tell the real ones from the invented ones. So the answer had to be something a model cannot produce: a hole in the ground with coordinates, a filing with a date, a disclosure a government compels. Every account here is discovered by measuring physical terrain. The language model is only ever allowed to write sentences out of facts that ordinary code already fetched.

Model
The only place a model is trusted
It receives a numbered fact list and writes four to seven sentences. It is told it has no other knowledge. It never chooses an account, a person, a score or a number.
Decision
Eleven hard gates. Does it pass?
No placeholder, no banned phrase, zero em dashes, correct length, two cited facts, one interest-based ask, language-correct readability, reader-centred and signed, every numeral traceable to a source, the account name spelled correctly, one language throughout, and a named peer with no misattributed figure.
fail  /  pass
Refusal
Rejected drafts are displayed
Up to four repairs, each told exactly which gate failed. If all four fail, that is recorded as a gap rather than resolved by lowering the bar. Showing the rejects proves the machine wrote them.

What it cost me to learn, and what is still open
The failure that shaped the critic
An early draft claimed "27 incidentes de seguridad". No such number exists. The model had turned "the word safety appears 27 times" into 27 incidents. Another inverted spend into saving. Both passed the original gates, because those gates checked that facts were cited, not that the paraphrase was faithful.
  • Counts are now withheld from the writer entirely
  • Quotable outcomes are a fixed closed list
  • Every numeral is checked against the permitted set
Where it hits a wall
The brief asked to be shown the walls. These are real and each has a stated fix.
  • The anchor has the thinnest contact surface of any account, so its own leadership page yields no operations seat
  • Corporate mail gateways answer for the gateway, so no email can be verified without probing, which we refuse to do
  • The free model tier allows 8,000 tokens a minute, which is why generation is paced rather than parallel
  • 365 of 389 salt ponds carry no operator tag, so proximity inference is required and is labelled
The honest weak point
The money case mixes three kinds of number and labels each one. A contracted crew day rate is not published by anybody, so it is carried as a wide band marked unsourced, excluded from anything an email may assert, and editable by the reader.
  • Published: FlytBase's own USD 70–80k phase one figure, linked
  • Derived: docks, crew-days, person-days out of hazard
  • Operator's own: day rate, value of an hour of downtime
